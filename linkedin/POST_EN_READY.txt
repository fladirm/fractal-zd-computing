533,208,060 zero-product relations. No 533-million-edge lookup table.

The count refers to the defined two-term assessor atlas in K2048.

I've been working on a computational structure built from the Cayley–Dickson algebras: a signed, recursively addressable forest of zero-divisor relations.

The interesting part isn't just compression. A simple tensor factorization can already make some large graphs cheap to apply. Here the algebra also gives us an exact four-address edge predicate, fourfold genealogical inheritance, newborn edges at every doubling, and higher-order information that a yes/no edge cannot encode.

A concrete example: two nonzero expressions multiply to zero, yet introducing a third factor produces a nonzero associator. This is why an ordinary binary adjacency is an incomplete representation of the algebra.

I packaged the research as two independent computer-science manuscripts, Rust/Python references, and an interactive HTML demonstration that runs offline with a double click.

Try the four scenes: address oracle, controlled failure/recovery, hidden associator channel, and edge birth across algebraic generations.
